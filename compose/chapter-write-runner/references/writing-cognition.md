# Writing Cognition

What a lens tells the writer about how its chapter reads. One set per
lens, supplied by the stage's section-form registry.

## Definition

1. Writing cognition is a lens's presentation knowledge: how a reader
   moves through the chapter, which carriers may hold it, and how it is
   expressed. It is applied at write time and never stored on a fact.
2. `reading_axis` is the chapter's abstract narrative axis — how it is
   read, not a content outline.
3. `presentation` holds the lens's carrier mechanisms: `guidance` in
   plain language; `allowed` entries, each naming a carrier, its
   structure, and the `when` under which it applies; and `forbidden`
   carriers or structures.
4. `expression` holds manner-of-expression constraints as `required` and
   `forbidden` lists. It shapes wording, not substance.

## Judgment

1. Mechanisms constrain the result; they are not steps to run. Choosing
   among `allowed` follows `when` and the facts, and the choice is not an
   artifact.
2. Stage domain (cognitive frame, expression conventions, vocabulary) and
   author role (expressive tendency) are soft attention beside the lens's
   cognition, never a substitute for it.
